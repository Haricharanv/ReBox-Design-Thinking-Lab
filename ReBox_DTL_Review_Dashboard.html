👥 Focused Group Interviews

Group discussion sessions conducted with clusters of stakeholders to extract collective insights.

🏠 Resident Group Session (8 participants)

Setting: Common area of Bellandur apartment complex, Bangalore

Duration: 45 minutes

Key collective insights:

  • "We all do the same thing — pile boxes on the balcony for days, then throw them."
  • Group consensus: A bin in the basement would be used by "literally everyone"
  • Debate on incentives: younger members wanted app rewards, older members said "just make it easy"
  • One member suggested the bin should be "right next to the lift" for maximum adoption

🏪 Vendor Cluster Session (5 shopkeepers)

Setting: Tea stall near Agara Market, Bangalore

Duration: 30 minutes

Key collective insights:

  • All 5 agreed: branded logos are "not an issue at all" — cost matters
  • "If boxes come from 200m away instead of 20km, I'll take all of them"
  • Disagreement on pricing: 3 said ₹2–5 is fair, 2 said "it should be free"
  • Fruit sellers need medium boxes most; kirana owners prefer large boxes

🎯 Defined Problem & HMW Statement

Root cause analysis (5 Whys + Fishbone) converging into a single actionable HMW statement.

5 Whys Analysis

  • Why landfill? → Thrown in dry waste.
  • Why throw? → No space, no alternative (80%).
  • Why no alternative? → Irregular Kabadiwala.
  • Why no redirection? → Weight-based selling to pulp.
  • ROOT: System optimized for RECYCLING not REUSE.

POV Statements

Shopper: Urban residents who shop online frequently NEED a zero-friction way to dispose of packaging BECAUSE space constraints force rapid disposal while eco-guilt makes them wish for better.

Vendor: Local vendors NEED consistent, hyper-local affordable boxes BECAUSE new packaging at ₹20–35/box consumes 10–15% of margins.

HMW Statement

"How Might We intercept structurally intact e-commerce boxes at the apartment level and redirect them to local vendors who need them — making disposal effortless for residents and supply reliable for shopkeepers?"

🧠 Brain Activities

Three distinct ideation exercises to push creative boundaries.

🧨 Brain Dump

Method: 5-minute unfiltered individual idea dump on sticky notes.

Output: 47 raw ideas generated across all team members. Key themes: bin placement, incentive systems, vendor notification, quality control, gamification.

Top ideas surfaced: "Bin near lift," "WhatsApp-only interface," "Box condition stickers," "Carbon points."

🔗 Brain Wired

Method: Connecting unrelated concepts to force novel solutions. We paired "Uber" + "Cardboard" and "Instagram" + "Recycling."

Output:

  • "Uber for Boxes" → On-demand vendor pickup alerts
  • "Instagram for Recycling" → Visual eco-impact stories shared on WhatsApp status
  • "Swiggy for Packaging" → Hyperlocal delivery matching

🚶 Brain Walk

Method: Physical walk around HSR Layout observing real vendor and apartment behavior.

Output:

  • Noticed vendors stacking branded e-com boxes already
  • Saw apartment dry waste bins overflowing with cardboard
  • Observed guards sorting boxes informally
  • Insight: Guards are natural "system operators"

💀 Worst Possible Idea + Challenge Assumptions

Reverse brainstorming: design the WORST solution, then flip insights into design principles.

🗑️ The Worst Idea

  • Require users to download a 200MB app
  • Force them to weigh each box on a postal scale
  • Schedule a 2-hour pickup window they must stay home for
  • Charge residents ₹50/box for "eco-processing"
  • Print a 3-page form for each drop-off

✅ Flipped Design Principles

  • ❌ 200MB app → ✅ Zero app download (WhatsApp only)
  • ❌ Weigh each box → ✅ Just drop, no measurement needed
  • ❌ 2-hour window → ✅ 24/7 bin access, drop anytime
  • ❌ Charge ₹50 → ✅ Free for residents, vendor pays ₹5
  • ❌ 3-page form → ✅ One QR scan, 3 seconds

Challenged Assumption: "People will download a new app for recycling." Our data showed zero tolerance for app downloads. The worst-idea exercise confirmed our layered approach: physical bin first, WhatsApp bot second, app never forced.

🌪️ Storm Techniques

Four storm-based ideation methods applied to push solution boundaries.

🏃 Body Storm

Activity: Team members physically enacted the box drop-off journey in a real apartment basement — walking from flat to lift to bin location to guard desk.

Insight: The bin MUST be visible from the lift lobby. If it requires walking to a different section, adoption drops. "Out of sight = out of mind."

🎮 Game Storm

Activity: Designed a points-based game: residents earn "ReBox Points" per drop, unlock badges ("Green Hero," "Box Champion"), and compete on a society leaderboard.

Insight: Gamification works for young users (20–30) but older users (40+) prefer intrinsic motivation ("it's the right thing") over points.

🃏 Cheat Storm

Activity: Asked: "What if we could cheat the laws of physics/economics?" Generated impossible-but-inspiring ideas.

Ideas: "What if Amazon pre-paid ₹5 reverse deposit per box?" → Led to exploring EPR credit integration. "What if boxes teleported to vendors?" → Led to the WhatsApp instant-alert system.

👥 Crowd Storm

Activity: Posted the problem statement on our class WhatsApp group (45 students) and collected 23 unique suggestions in 48 hours.

Top suggestions adopted: "Partner with society maintenance staff," "Add a box flattener tool to the bin," "Monthly vendor 'thank you' card to top contributors."

✅ Final Solution: The Layered Circular System

A 3-layer solution designed for universal adoption across all tech-literacy levels.

Layer 1: Physical Green Bin

Dedicated corrugated-only bin in apartment basements. Zero tech required. Users like Swarna (45) who values simplicity.

Layer 2: WhatsApp Bot

QR scan opens WhatsApp chat. Auto-logs drops, alerts vendors, tracks impact. No app download. Core interface.

Layer 3: Dashboard App

Future vision: full app for power users. Shopper/Vendor/Guard portals with analytics and leaderboards.

Test Phase

Validating the prototype through user feedback, iteration, and knowledge assessment.

💬 Feedback Collection

Structured feedback gathered from prototype demonstrations to 3 stakeholder groups.

🏠 Resident Feedback (12 users)

  • Positive: "This is exactly what my society needs" (10/12)
  • Positive: "WhatsApp is perfect — no new app to learn" (11/12)
  • Concern: "Will the bin get dirty or smelly?" (3/12)
  • Suggestion: "Add a small flattener tool attached to the bin" (2/12)

🏪 Vendor Feedback (8 shopkeepers)

  • Positive: "₹5 per box? I'll take 50 a week" (7/8)
  • Positive: "WhatsApp alert when boxes ready — very convenient" (8/8)
  • Concern: "What if sizes I need aren't available?" (4/8)
  • Suggestion: "Allow me to specify size requests" (5/8)

👮 Guard Feedback (3 guards)

  • Positive: "Simple verification — just click one button" (3/3)
  • Concern: "Who cleans the bin area?" (2/3)
  • Concern: "What if vendor comes when I'm not at desk?" (1/3)
  • Suggestion: "Fixed pickup time slots would help" (2/3)

Net Promoter Score (NPS): When asked "How likely are you to recommend ReBox to your society?" — 87% scored 8 or above (out of 10). This indicates strong product-market fit.
