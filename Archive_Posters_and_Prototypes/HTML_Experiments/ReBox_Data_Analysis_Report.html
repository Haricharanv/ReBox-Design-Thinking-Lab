Data Analysis & Insights Report

Phase 2: Empathize & Define | ReBox Project (May 2026)

This report synthesizes the primary data collected from 31 local shopkeepers and 10 urban e-commerce shoppers. Our objective is to transition from raw survey counts to deep, actionable human insights regarding the financial and behavioral dimensions of corrugated box waste.

1. The Financial Burden on Local Vendors

Local vendors (Kirana stores, fruit sellers, bakeries) form the backbone of urban neighborhoods. However, packaging costs are severely impacting their profit margins.

Figure 1.1: Distribution of monthly spending on packaging across 31 surveyed vendors.

Insight: The Invisible Tax on MSMEs

Over 77% of the surveyed shopkeepers spend more than ₹500 monthly on boxes, with 45% spending between ₹2,000 and ₹5,000+. This represents a massive operational drain. For a small fruit vendor making a slim margin per kg, spending ₹25 on a single new corrugated box directly diminishes their daily take-home income.

Figure 1.2: Frequency of requiring corrugated boxes for business operations.

Furthermore, 84% of vendors require boxes either "every day" or "a few times a week". The demand is high and continuous, yet the supply is entirely dependent on expensive new purchases or unpredictable scrap dealers.

The core of the ReBox concept lies in identifying a massive market inefficiency between the scrap value of a box and its functional value.

Insight: Profitability in Reuse

Currently, consumers sell used boxes to scrap dealers for roughly ₹3–₹5 per kg (which equates to pennies per box), destined for pulping. Meanwhile, vendors buy equivalent new boxes for ₹25. By implementing a direct C2B (Consumer-to-Business) drop-off bin system at a nominal ₹5 fee, ReBox bridges a ₹20 market gap per unit, turning environmental waste into direct financial relief for local businesses.

3. Vendor Willingness & Brand Neutrality

A critical assumption to test was whether vendors would accept second-hand boxes that display corporate branding (e.g., Amazon, Flipkart, Myntra logos).

Figure 3.1: Vendor willingness to utilize second-hand e-commerce packaging.

Insight: "A Box is a Box"

An overwhelming 83.8% of shopkeepers are willing to use second-hand boxes (45.2% "definitely", 38.7% "if condition is good"). Audio interviews corroborated that end-consumers do not penalize local vendors for using Amazon-branded boxes to carry groceries. The primary deal-breakers for vendors are physical defects: wetness, bad odors, and severe tearing.

4. The Urban Shopper's Dilemma: Clutter vs. Guilt

Transitioning to the supply side, we analyzed the behavior of 10 urban online shoppers living in apartments.

Figure 4.1: Time taken by urban consumers to dispose of e-commerce boxes.

The data reveals that 50% of users hoard boxes ("piling up") before ultimately throwing them out. This creates a psychological tension between wanting to act sustainably and needing to clear physical space in small urban apartments.

Insight: Convenience Trumps Guilt

While shoppers experience "eco-guilt," their ultimate disposal method defaults to municipal dry waste because the traditional recycling route (waiting for a kabadiwala) is highly inconvenient. Therefore, ReBox's primary value proposition to the consumer is not just environmental impact, but immediate space reclamation via extreme convenience (the basement drop-off bin).

5. Final Strategic Recommendations

Based on the data synthesis, the ReBox implementation strategy must adopt the following parameters:

  • Focus on Quality Control: Since vendors reject wet or smelly boxes, the ReBox collection bins must be designed with weather-proofing and strict visual instructions to only deposit dry, intact cardboard.
  • Pitch the Financials: When onboarding shopkeepers, the marketing pitch should entirely revolve around the "₹30,000+ Annual Savings," positioning ReBox as a cost-cutting tool rather than just a green initiative.
  • Hyper-Local Network: To maintain the ₹5 nominal cost, logistics must be negligible. Drop-off bins in apartments must be mapped to vendors within a maximum 1–2 km radius.
