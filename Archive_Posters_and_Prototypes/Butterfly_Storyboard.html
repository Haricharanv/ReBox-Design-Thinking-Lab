🦋 Butterfly Cycle
× Design Thinking

Just as a butterfly transforms through distinct stages of metamorphosis, the Design Thinking process guides ideas through a powerful journey of transformation — from raw empathy to elegant, human-centred solutions.

↓ Scroll to Explore ↓

Stage 1 · The Egg

Empathize

"Every great design begins with a seed of understanding."

A butterfly's journey starts with a tiny egg laid carefully on a leaf — chosen specifically for the caterpillar's survival. Similarly, Design Thinking begins with Empathize: observing, listening, and immersing yourself in the user's world.

🔗 The Analogy: The mother butterfly selects the right leaf — just as designers must find the right problem by deeply understanding user needs, pain points, and aspirations before anything else begins.
Stage 2 · The Caterpillar

Define

"Consume knowledge. Grow with purpose."

The caterpillar eats voraciously — gathering nutrients and energy essential for its transformation. In Design Thinking, the Define stage is about synthesizing all the empathy data into a clear, actionable problem statement.

🔗 The Analogy: Just as the caterpillar processes every leaf to fuel growth, designers process every insight — sorting, clustering, and refining observations into a focused Point of View (POV) statement.
Stage 3 · The Chrysalis

Ideate

"Transformation happens in stillness."

Inside the chrysalis, the caterpillar's body dissolves and reorganizes into something entirely new. This hidden, radical transformation mirrors Ideation — where wild brainstorming, mind-mapping, and creative exploration happen.

🔗 The Analogy: The chrysalis is a safe space for radical change — just like ideation sessions are judgement-free zones where every idea, no matter how wild, contributes to breakthrough solutions.
Stage 4 · The Emergence

Prototype

"Bring the invisible into the visible."

The butterfly emerges — fragile, wet, wings crumpled. It isn't perfect yet, but it's real. This is the Prototype phase: turning the best ideas into tangible, testable artifacts — rough, fast, and iterative.

🔗 The Analogy: The newly emerged butterfly must pump fluid into its wings before it can fly — just as prototypes must be refined through hands-on iteration before they become viable solutions.
Stage 5 · The Flight

Test

"Spread your wings and learn from the wind."

Finally, the butterfly takes flight — encountering real wind, real predators, real flowers. In Design Thinking, Testing means putting the prototype in front of real users and learning from their genuine feedback.

🔗 The Analogy: A butterfly adapts its flight path based on wind and obstacles — just as designers iterate on solutions based on user feedback, sometimes looping back to earlier stages to refine the design.
