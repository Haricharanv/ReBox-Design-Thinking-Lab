15 MILLION BOXES DESTROYED DAILY!

BENGALURU — An exhaustive field investigation by researchers Hari Charan V, Anjali Kumari, and Supriya has uncovered a massive flaw in the urban e-commerce pipeline. Despite being structurally perfect, millions of cardboard boxes are crushed daily, while local vendors suffer from skyrocketing packaging costs.

"I feel guilty... but boxes take up too much space. I prioritize a clean house over the guilt."
— Priya, 28, IT Professional

THE SHOPPER POV: Urban residents need a zero-friction way to dispose of packaging because space constraints force rapid disposal. Eco-guilt exists, but convenience consistently overrides it. 80% of surveyed shoppers cited "clutter" as the primary reason for throwing boxes in the dry waste bin.

Vendors Demand Local Supply

THE VENDOR POV: Local kirana owners need a consistent, hyper-local supply of affordable boxes because purchasing new packaging at ₹20-35 per box consumes 10-15% of their thin margins. The scrap ecosystem currently channels all used boxes to pulp factories instead of back to vendors.

"A box is a box. My customer doesn't care if it says Amazon. If it's intact, I'll take it."
— Ramesh, 45, Kirana Owner

Thrown in dry waste.

System optimized for RECYCLING (destroying) instead of REUSE (redirecting).
