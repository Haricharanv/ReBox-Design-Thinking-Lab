# ═══════════════════════════════════════
# SLIDE 5 — KEY DATA FINDINGS (SHOPPERS)
# ═══════════════════════════════════════

**Title:** Key Findings — Online Shoppers

**Finding 1: Space is the #1 Pain Point**
- 80%+ of shoppers cited clutter / "no storage space" as the primary reason for quick disposal
- Most discard boxes within 3–7 days

**Finding 2: Eco-Guilt is Present but Overridden**
- Users feel guilty throwing structurally perfect boxes
- But convenience always wins over guilt
- "I prioritize a clean house over the guilt" — Priya, 26

**Finding 3: 100% Would Use a Basement Drop-off Bin**
- Zero-effort, nearby collection point resonated universally

**Finding 4: Incentive Preferences Split by Age**
- Young (20–30): Prefer cashback, app rewards
- Older (40+): Prefer doorstep pickup, no apps
- This led to our LAYERED solution approach

# ═══════════════════════════════════════
# SLIDE 6 — KEY DATA FINDINGS (SHOPKEEPERS)
# ═══════════════════════════════════════

**Title:** Key Findings — Shopkeepers & Vendors

**Finding 1: Packaging is a Major Cost Burden**
- Vendors spend ₹20–₹35 per NEW box
- Monthly spend: ₹500 to ₹5,000+ depending on shop type
- Packaging is the 2nd largest operational cost after inventory

**Finding 2: Brand Neutrality = Zero Barrier**
- 96% said Amazon/Flipkart logos are "No problem at all" or "Slightly, but okay"
- Only requirement: box must be clean, dry, and structurally intact

**Finding 3: High Willingness to Adopt**
- 90%+ would use second-hand boxes
- Preferred price: ₹1–₹5 per box (vs ₹20–₹35 new) or free
- Only 1 out of 25+ rejected the concept entirely

**Finding 4: Preferred Logistics — Self-Pickup from Nearby Point**
- Most vendors willing to go pick up boxes if collection point is close
- Some (bakeries, large stores) prefer delivery to shop

# SLIDE 11 — ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS (5 WHYS)
# ═══════════════════════════════════════

**Title:** Root Cause Analysis — The 5 Whys

**Problem:** Perfectly good e-commerce boxes are destroyed while nearby vendors pay premium for new ones.

| # | WHY? | ANSWER (Evidence-Based) |
|---|------|------------------------|
| 1 | Why do good boxes end up in landfills? | Because residents throw them in dry waste bins |
| 2 | Why do residents throw them away? | Because they lack space AND have no easy alternative (8/10 shoppers confirmed) |
| 3 | Why is there no easy alternative? | Because kabadiwala visits are irregular, and no local collection system exists |
| 4 | Why doesn't the kabadiwala redirect boxes to vendors? | Because he sells by WEIGHT to pulp factories — crushing is more profitable than preserving |
| 5 | Why don't vendors collect directly from apartments? | Because NO organized system connects box-generators (residents) with box-needers (vendors) |

### 🎯 ROOT CAUSE:
> The scrap ecosystem is optimized for RECYCLING (destroy → pulp → new box), not REUSE (redirect intact box). There is zero infrastructure connecting apartment residents with local vendors at the neighborhood level.

# ═══════════════════════════════════════
# SLIDE 12 — ROOT CAUSE: FISHBONE DIAGRAM
# ═══════════════════════════════════════

**Title:** Fishbone (Ishikawa) Diagram — Why Boxes Go to Waste

**PROBLEM (head of fish):** E-commerce boxes are destroyed instead of reused locally

**Category 1 — PEOPLE**
- Residents prioritize convenience over eco-guilt
- Vendors don't know apartments generate box surplus
- Kabadiwala is incentivized to destroy, not preserve

**Category 2 — PROCESS**
- No organized collection mechanism at apartment level
- Scrap pipeline: Consumer → Kabadiwala → Pulp Factory (linear, not circular)
- Municipal waste system mixes cardboard with other waste

**Category 3 — ECONOMICS**
- Scrap value: ₹3/box vs. Reuse value: ₹20–35/box
- Kabadiwala earns more from weight-based selling to factories
- Vendors can't compete with factory bulk-buying prices

**Category 4 — INFRASTRUCTURE**
- No collection bins for reusable boxes in apartments
- No matchmaking platform between supply (residents) and demand (vendors)
- No tracking/quality assurance system for reused boxes

# SLIDE 13 — PROBLEM DEFINITION
# ═══════════════════════════════════════

**Title:** Problem Definition

**The Macro Problem:**
India ships ~15 million e-commerce parcels per day. Each generates at least one corrugated box. These structurally perfect boxes enter a broken pipeline — consumer → kabadiwala → pulp factory → DESTROYED. Meanwhile, 12–15 million local vendors pay ₹20–35 per NEW box.

**Key Insight: REUSE ≠ RECYCLING**
- Current system RECYCLES (destroys box → makes pulp → manufactures new box)
- ReBox proposes REUSE (redirects intact box directly to someone who needs it)
- A corrugated box can be reused 5–7 times before losing integrity
- Each reuse avoids the full manufacturing carbon footprint
- A used box worth ₹3 as scrap is worth ₹20–35 as packaging to a vendor

**The Value Gap:**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Scrap value of 1 used box | ₹2–4.50 (at ₹7–9/kg) |
| Cost of 1 new box for vendor | ₹20–35 |
| Price gap (ReBox opportunity) | ₹15–30 per box |
| Potential savings for vendor | 80–100% reduction |
