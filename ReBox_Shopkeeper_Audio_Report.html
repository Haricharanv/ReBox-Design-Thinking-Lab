Key Insights

Themes extracted from all 8 recordings.

1. The Cost Burden is Real

  • Vendors spend ₹20–₹35 per box when buying new from wholesale markets.
  • A single kirana store spending ₹6,000/month on boxes — that's ₹72,000/year on cardboard alone.
  • Packaging is the second-largest operational cost after inventory for small vendors.

2. Brand Neutrality = Zero Barrier

  • Every vendor confirmed: customers don't care about Amazon/Flipkart logos on boxes.
  • The only requirements are: clean, dry, and structurally intact.
  • This eliminates the need for expensive relabeling or box cleaning before redistribution.

3. The Supply Chain Gap

  • Currently, vendors rely on distant wholesale markets for new boxes.
  • Scrap dealers buy used boxes by weight and sell to pulp factories — vendors can't compete with factory prices.
  • There is no organized, local source of reusable boxes — this is the exact gap ReBox fills.

4. Preferred Logistics Model

  • Vendors prefer a daily or on-demand pickup system over weekly bulk delivery.
  • Their own storage is limited — they can't stockpile.
  • A nearby apartment-node collection point that vendors can visit on their own schedule is the ideal model.

5. The Scrap Economics Gap (Industry-Validated)

  • Cardboard scrap rate in urban India: ₹7–9 per kg (ScrapRates.in, 2026). A single box weighs ~300–500g, so its scrap value is just ₹2–4.50.
  • But vendors pay ₹20–35 for a NEW box of the same size — a ₹15–30 value gap per box.
  • The kabadiwala system handles 30–60% of recyclable waste (CSE India) but channels everything to pulp factories — destroying reusable boxes.
  • No existing startup or initiative in India connects apartment residents directly to local vendors for box reuse. Recykal, Saahas Zero Waste, and TheKabadiwala.com all send boxes to recycling, not reuse.
  • EPR rules for paper/cardboard packaging became effective April 1, 2026 (MoEF&CC) — creating regulatory demand for reuse infrastructure like ReBox.
