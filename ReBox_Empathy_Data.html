Part 1: Primary Empathy Interviews

Interview 1: The Urban Shopper (Priya, 26, IT Professional)

Q (Interviewer): Walk me through what happens when an Amazon package arrives.

A (Priya): I open the box, get my item, and throw the cardboard in the corner of my balcony. I tell myself I'll give it to the Kabadiwala, but he only comes once a month. The boxes just pile up and look ugly. Eventually, I get frustrated, flatten them all, and just dump them in the apartment's 'Dry Waste' bin.

Q: Does throwing them in the dry waste bother you?

A: Extremely. They are perfectly strong boxes. But I live in a 2BHK, I don't have the space to hoard them. I feel guilty, but I prioritize a clean house over the guilt.

Q: If there was a designated "Box Donation Bin" downstairs, would you use it?

A: Only if it's completely easy. I won't use an app to schedule a pickup for 3 boxes. If I can just scan a barcode on my way to work and leave it, absolutely.

Interview 2: The Local Vendor (Ramesh, 45, Fruit Seller)

Q (Interviewer): How much do you spend on packaging to pack apples and mangoes?

A (Ramesh): It's hurting my business. A single good corrugated box costs me ₹20 to ₹35 depending on the size. I buy 50 boxes a week. That's up to ₹1,500 just on cardboard. My profit margin is already low.

Q: Would you use second-hand Amazon/Myntra boxes if you got them for free or ₹5?

A: 100%. A box is a box. As long as it is not wet or torn, my customers don't care if it has an Amazon smile on it. But I don't know anyone who gives them. Scrap dealers sell them directly to pulp factories by weight.

Part 2: Empathy Mapping

Empathy Map: The Urban Shopper

🗣 Says

  • "I'll give it to the scrap guy later."
  • "These boxes take up too much space."
  • "I don't have time to use a recycling app."

💭 Thinks

  • "I am contributing to environmental waste."
  • "There must be someone who needs these."
  • "Why is recycling so inconvenient?"

🏃 Does

  • Hoards boxes in the balcony until they become an eyesore.
  • Flattens them and throws them in municipal dry waste.
  • Defaults to the easiest disposal method available.

❤ Feels

  • Guilty about wasting good materials.
  • Frustrated by the clutter in her small apartment.
  • Apathetic toward complex recycling incentive programs.

Empathy Map: The Local Vendor

🗣 Says

  • "Cardboard prices keep going up."
  • "My margins are disappearing."
  • "I have to pass the packaging cost to the customer."

💭 Thinks

  • "I wish I could get cheap, strong boxes locally."
  • "The scrap dealer charges too much for old boxes."
  • "How can I cut my operational costs without losing quality?"

🏃 Does

  • Travels to wholesale markets to buy new boxes in bulk.
  • Stores excess box inventory, tying up his working capital.
  • Reuses boxes until they completely break apart.

❤ Feels

  • Stressed about rising wholesale supplier rates.
  • Helpless regarding his dependency on raw paper prices.
  • Pragmatic & happy to use branded second-hand boxes if they lower costs.

Part 3: Point of View (POV) & How Might We (HMW) Statements

Shopper POV: Urban apartment residents who shop online frequently need an easy way to dispose of their accumulating e-commerce packaging because space constraints force rapid disposal, while eco-guilt makes them wish for a better option than the garbage bin.

Vendor POV: Local kirana store owners and small vendors need a consistent, local supply of affordable corrugated boxes because purchasing new packaging at ₹20–35/box is consuming 10–15% of their already thin margins, and the current scrap ecosystem channels all used boxes to pulp factories instead of back to vendors who could reuse them.

KEY INSIGHT: REUSE ≠ RECYCLING

The current system recycles boxes (destroying them to make pulp for new boxes). ReBox proposes reuse — redirecting intact boxes directly. A corrugated box can be reused 5–7 times before losing structural integrity. Each reuse avoids the full manufacturing carbon footprint. A used box worth ₹3 as scrap is worth ₹20–35 as packaging to a vendor.

"How Might We collect intact e-commerce boxes at the apartment level and give them to local vendors who need them — making it easy for residents and reliable for shopkeepers?"

Part 6: Shopkeeper & Vendor Audio Insights

Insights synthesized from 8 WhatsApp audio recordings captured during field visits to local vendors on 2nd May 2026.

Key Findings from Vendor Recordings

  • Cost Barrier: Small vendors (Kirana stores, fruit sellers) spend ₹20–₹35 per new corrugated box. Packaging is their second-largest operational cost after inventory.
  • Brand Neutrality: 100% of interviewed vendors confirmed customers don't care about Amazon/Flipkart branding — they only care the box is clean and sturdy.
  • Supply Gap: Currently, vendors either buy expensive new boxes from wholesale markets or scavenge from scrap dealers who charge a premium. There is no organized, local source of reusable boxes.
  • Willingness to Adopt: All vendors expressed willingness to use second-hand boxes if supplied at ₹5 or free. One vendor said: "A box is a box. My customer doesn't look at who sent it before."
  • Preferred Model: Vendors prefer a daily or on-demand drop-off system over bulk weekly deliveries, since their own storage is limited.
