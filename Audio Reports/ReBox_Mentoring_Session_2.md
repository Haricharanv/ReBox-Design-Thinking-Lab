# ReBox Project - Mentoring Session Part 2

**Mentor:** Prof. Chandrani Chakravorty
**Team Members:** Hari Charan V, Anjali Kumari, Supriya

### 1. Session Overview

This mentoring session with Prof. Chandrani Chakravorty provided invaluable guidance for the ReBox project, emphasizing critical aspects of fieldwork, data collection, and overall project methodology. The discussion focused on practical strategies to ensure comprehensive data gathering and a robust foundation for developing effective solutions. Prof. Chakravorty encouraged the team to adopt a proactive, empathetic, and resourceful approach to their research, highlighting the importance of direct engagement with end-users and a multi-faceted data collection strategy.

### 2. Constructive Feedback & Guidance

*   **Importance of Documentation:** The team was guided to maintain meticulous records of all project activities, including meeting minutes and data collection efforts. This ensures a clear history of progress and decisions.
*   **Strategic Fieldwork Execution:** Prof. Chakravorty stressed the necessity of direct engagement for the ReBox project, as it requires in-depth understanding from end-users. She encouraged expanding the scope of respondents beyond their immediate academic circle, suggesting interactions with other department faculty, neighbors, and local businesses like shops and eateries, which often generate reusable materials.
*   **Diverse Data Collection Methods:** Recognizing that Google Forms might not be universally suitable, the team received guidance on diversifying their data collection strategies. This includes:
    *   **Printed Questionnaires:** Preparing physical questionnaires for respondents to fill out, ensuring signatures are collected for authenticity.
    *   **Audio Recording:** As an alternative to video, voice recordings were suggested, provided permission is obtained.
    *   **Brochures/Pamphlets with Surveys:** Utilizing informative brochures in public spaces (like malls or markets) that include a brief survey for quick feedback.
    *   **Assistance with Forms:** Offering to help respondents fill out forms if they face difficulties in writing.
*   **Professionalism and Trust-Building:** The team was advised to always wear their ID cards during fieldwork to establish credibility and trust. A clear, polite introduction script was provided, outlining their affiliation, project purpose, and a request for assistance, which is crucial for successful interactions.
*   **Empathy and Adaptability in Communication:** Prof. Chakravorty emphasized the importance of understanding respondents' perspectives and comfort levels. This includes:
    *   **Language Preference:** Allowing respondents to answer in their preferred local language (e.g., Kannada) and providing translated questionnaires.
    *   **Sensitivity to Topics:** Approaching sensitive topics with empathy, acknowledging that respondents might use euphemisms, and creating a comfortable environment for honest feedback.
    *   **Varied Engagement:** Setting realistic expectations that not all respondents will engage at the same level, and being prepared for diverse responses.
*   **Proactive Problem-Solving & Critical Thinking:** The mentor encouraged the team to take ownership of generating ideas and solutions, rather than waiting for instructions. The emphasis was on understanding the practical problems from the users' viewpoint, as AI tools like ChatGPT cannot provide real-world insights for their specific project.
*   **Linking Fieldwork to Project Success:** It was highlighted that thorough data collection is foundational for developing a robust prototype and presenting well-supported conclusions. The number of people reached and the quality of information gathered will be key evaluation criteria for their final presentation.

### 3. Actionable Next Steps for the Team

1.  **Prepare and Document:**
    *   Create a clear, concise introduction script for approaching respondents.
    *   Prepare printed questionnaires and their Kannada translations.
    *   Design informative brochures or pamphlets that can serve as survey tools.
    *   Ensure all team members have their ID cards for fieldwork.

2.  **Execute Fieldwork Systematically:**
    *   Set a target of reaching a minimum of 30 respondents per team member by next week.
    *   Actively engage with potential respondents from diverse groups: other department faculty, neighbors, local households, and businesses (e.g., vegetable shops, eateries).
    *   Collect tangible proof of interactions (e.g., signed questionnaires or filled pamphlets).
    *   Politely ask for permission for video recording; if declined, request audio recording or offer printed forms.
    *   Offer assistance to respondents who might struggle with writing.

3.  **Analyze and Plan Proactively:**
    *   Begin analyzing collected data to clearly identify core problems from the end-users' perspective.
    *   Brainstorm and discuss potential solutions and unique features for the ReBox project based on the collected insights.
    *   Develop a detailed plan for the next project phase, considering how current fieldwork will directly inform future development.

4.  **Embrace Self-Initiative:**
    *   Take the lead in proposing creative ideas and strategies for the project.
    *   Maintain consistent progress and effort, demonstrating a self-driven approach to project completion.

By implementing these actionable steps and adhering to the guidance provided, the team is well-positioned to gather comprehensive data, develop innovative solutions, and successfully advance the ReBox project.
