ReBox Project: Affinity Diagram

Synthesizing research data from online shoppers, local vendors, and field observations to identify core problems and opportunities.

📦 Shopper Behaviors & Needs

"Boxes take up too much space. My balcony gets cluttered very fast."
"I feel eco-guilt throwing them away, but convenience always wins out."
Boxes are usually discarded within 3-7 days directly into the dry waste bin.
100% of users said they would use a zero-effort basement drop-off bin.
Incentives matter: Young users prefer app rewards, older users prefer doorstep pickup.

🏪 Vendor Behaviors & Needs

Packaging is the 2nd largest cost. Vendors spend ₹500 - ₹5,000+/month on new boxes.
"Brand logos don't matter at all as long as the box is intact." (96% agreed)
High willingness to pay ₹1-₹5 for a good quality second-hand box.
Currently traveling 20+ km to wholesale markets to buy expensive new boxes.
Vendors are willing to pick up used boxes if the collection point is nearby.

♻️ System & Infrastructure Gaps

The scrap ecosystem destroys intact boxes for pulp (recycling) instead of reusing them.
Zero organized collection mechanisms exist at the apartment level for reusable packaging.
No local connection or matchmaking between residents (supply) and vendors (demand).
Kabadiwalas are incentivized by weight, making crushing more profitable than preserving boxes.
The current waste pipeline is entirely linear (Consumer → Kabadiwala → Pulp Factory).

💰 Economic & Environmental Impact

Massive Value Gap: A used box is worth ₹3 as scrap, but ₹20-35 to a vendor.
A structurally sound corrugated box can be reused 5-7 times before it degrades.
India ships ~15 million e-commerce parcels per day, generating massive waste.
Reusing a box avoids the full manufacturing carbon footprint of a new one.
Paper packaging Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) regulations are creating a regulatory tailwind.
