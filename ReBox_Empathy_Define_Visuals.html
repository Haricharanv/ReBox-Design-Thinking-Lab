👥 User Personas

"The Convenience Seeker"

Priya, 28, IT Prof.

Goal: Rid balcony of clutter.

Behavior: 5-8 packages/month. Will use drop-bin, won't download an app.

"The Pragmatic Vendor"

Ramesh, 45, Kirana Owner

Goal: Source cheap packaging.

Behavior: Pays ₹25/new box. Doesn't care if box has an Amazon logo. Needs 30-50/mo.

🧠 Empathy Map: Shopper

Says

"These boxes take too much space... I'll give it to scrap guy later."

Thinks

"I'm contributing to waste. Someone must need these."

Does

Hoards boxes, then gives up and dumps in dry waste bin.

Feels

Guilty about waste. Frustrated by small apartment clutter.

Core Need: Frictionless, guilt-free disposal.

🧠 Empathy Map: Vendor

Says

"Cardboard prices keep rising... my margins are disappearing."

Thinks

"I wish I could get cheap, strong boxes locally."

Does

Buys expensive new boxes in bulk. Reuses until torn.

Feels

Stressed about raw paper prices. Helpless.

Core Need: Cheap, consistent box supply.

💡 POV Statements & Key Data

Shopper: Urban residents NEED zero-friction disposal BECAUSE space limits force rapid disposal, while eco-guilt makes them wish for a better option.

Vendor: Local kiranas NEED a hyper-local supply of affordable boxes BECAUSE new boxes (₹25) consume 10-15% of thin margins.

🔍 5 Whys (Root Cause)

1. Why landfill? Residents throw in dry waste.
⤵
2. Why throw away? No space + no easy alternative.
⤵
3. Why no alternative? Kabadiwala visits are irregular.
⤵
4. Why doesn't kabadiwala redirect? Sells by weight; crushing is easier.

🎯 ROOT CAUSE:

System is built to RECYCLE (destroy), not REUSE (redirect). No bridge exists between residents and local vendors.

"How Might We intercept intact boxes at the apartment level and redirect them to local vendors who need them — making disposal effortless for residents and supply reliable for shopkeepers?"
