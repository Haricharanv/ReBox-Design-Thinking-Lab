Phase: 2 – Empathy & Define

ReBox – Survey Question Analysis

Purpose: Evaluate whether questionnaires yield actionable insights or surface-level data

Overall Verdict

IMPORTANT FINDING

The current survey collects mostly data, not deep insights. Approximately 60–70% of the questions are framed as "Would you [use our solution]?" — that's desirability testing (Phase 5: Test), not empathy research (Phase 2).

The survey is a solid starting point but leans heavily toward quantitative data collection and solution validation rather than genuine empathy-driven insight discovery.

✅ Questions That Generate Real Insights

These questions tap into emotions, frustrations, and hidden behaviors — the gold standard for empathy research.

Questionnaire 1 – Urban Resident / Online Shopper

# Question Why It Works
Q4 "Do you feel guilty throwing away a good-condition cardboard box? Why?" Taps into emotional response — guilt is a powerful motivator and reveals values.
Q6 "What frustrates you the most about dealing with packaging waste in your flat or PG?" Uncovers latent pain points and unmet needs directly from experience.

Questionnaire 2 – Local Vendor / Small Business Owner

# Question Why It Works
Q4 "Are you satisfied with your current box supply in terms of cost, quality, and availability?" Opens up a multi-dimensional pain point (cost, quality, supply chain).
Q7 "What conditions would make you reject a donated box?" Reveals hidden deal-breakers and quality thresholds.

Questionnaire 3 – Apartment Complex / RWA Administrator

# Question Why It Works
Q2 "Is cardboard and packaging waste a growing problem...especially with quick-commerce?" Contextualizes the problem in the user's lived reality.
Q5 "What is your biggest concern about placing such a bin?" Surfaces real blockers — genuine fears like hygiene or pest attraction.
Q10 "Are there any BBMP regulations...that could block such an initiative?" Uncovers systemic barriers that could kill the project.

⚠️ Questions That Collect Data, Not Insights

These questions yield counts, yes/no answers, or hypothetical willingness.

Problem 1: Closed / Leading Questions

Question Issue
"If a drop-off bin was placed... would you use it?" (Q1.8) Binary Yes/No — you get a count, not a story.
"Would you scan a QR code... or is that too much effort?" (Q1.9) Leading — implies the answer and limits exploration.

Problem 2: Solution-First Framing

Question Issue
"Would you be willing to use an app or WhatsApp bot...?" (Q2.10) Tests a specific solution channel — premature for Phase 2.
"What would motivate you — reward points, UPI cashback...?" (Q1.10) Presents your ideas instead of letting users express their own motivations.

🔄 Recommended Replacements

Transform surface-level questions into story-driven, open-ended prompts.

Instead Of Ask This
"Would you use a drop-off bin?" "Walk me through what happens from the moment a delivery arrives to when the box finally leaves your home."
"Would you scan a QR code?" "What's the laziest thing you've ever done to get rid of packaging waste? No judgment."
"Tell me about the last time you needed a box urgently. Where did you go?" "Tell me about the last time you needed a box urgently. Where did you go? What was that experience like?"
"Would your RWA support ReBox?" "What's the last sustainability initiative your apartment tried? What happened to it — did it succeed or fizzle out? Why?"

💡 Key Recommendations

  • Reframe 5–6 closed questions per questionnaire into open-ended, story-driven prompts.
  • Move solution-testing questions to a separate Phase 5 validation survey.
  • Use the survey as a conversation guide for 1-on-1 interviews, not just a standalone form.
  • Add observation notes — focus on what people do, not just what they say.
  • Include a "surprise me" question: "Is there anything about cardboard waste I should have asked but didn't?"

📝 Retrospective: How We Addressed These Issues

TEAM REFLECTION (May 2026)

After completing the survey phase, we realized we jumped to conclusions too early in our questionnaire design. Several questions tested our solution (app, QR code, WhatsApp bot) rather than exploring the user's world openly.

  • Revised HMW Statement: We moved from "How might we make giving an Amazon box to a local vendor as easy as throwing it in the garbage chute?" to a general, two-sided approach: "How Might We collect intact e-commerce boxes at the apartment level and give them to local vendors who need them — making it easy for residents and reliable for shopkeepers?"
  • Expanded Ideation: Instead of committing to an app, we brainstormed 10+ solution concepts across low-tech (physical bins, notice boards), mid-tech (QR + WhatsApp), and high-tech (full app) categories — then evaluated each on friction, feasibility, vendor adoption, scalability, and inclusivity.
  • Secondary Research Added: We supplemented our primary data with macro context — India ships ~15M parcels/day (IBEF), cardboard scrap is ₹7–9/kg but new boxes cost ₹20–35 (a ₹15–30 value gap per box), no Indian startup does consumer-to-vendor reuse, and EPR rules for paper packaging became effective April 2026.
  • Layered Prototype: Our prototype evolved into a 3-layer system — (1) Physical bin for everyone, (2) QR + WhatsApp for tracking, (3) App for future scale — each layer mapped to specific user personas from our empathy research.
