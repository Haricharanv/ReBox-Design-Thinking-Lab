1. How Might We (HMW) Framing

The bridge from user pain points to solution generation.

The HMW Statement:

"How Might We intercept structurally intact e-commerce boxes at the apartment level and redirect them to local vendors who need them — making disposal effortless for residents and supply reliable for shopkeepers?"

This HMW statement was synthesized directly from Phase 2 insights. It addresses two primary user groups:

  • The Supply Side (Online Shoppers): Squeezed by space constraints, they feel "eco-guilt" throwing boxes away, but will default to dry waste bins unless the collection point is zero-friction and immediate.
  • The Demand Side (Local Vendors): Burdened by rising packaging costs (spending ₹20–₹35/new box), they are brand-neutral and highly receptive to second-hand boxes if they are sturdy and local.

2. Divergent Brainstorming

Generating a wide spectrum of ideas before narrowing down.

Our team conducted brainstorming sessions to explore various potential interventions. We categorized our ideas into three technology tiers to ensure viability and accessibility across age groups and budgets:

Tier 1: Low-Tech Options

Focuses on zero-cost, physical, and localized solutions.

  • Physical Green Bins: Dedicated cardboard-only collection bins placed in apartment lobbies/basements.
  • Signage & Pamphlets: Educational posters placed near bins showing acceptable box conditions.
  • WhatsApp Groups: Local society groups where residents post available box sizes and vendors claim them manually.

Tier 2: Mid-Tech Options

Leverages lightweight automation without requiring complex app installations.

  • QR-Enabled Bins: Bins with QR codes that, when scanned, open a direct chat interface with a WhatsApp Bot.
  • WhatsApp Bot: Automates pickup scheduling, alerts guards, and logs drops.
  • SMS Broadcasts: Simple text alerts to registered local vendors within 500m when bins reach capacity.

Tier 3: High-Tech Options

Scalable, data-driven, and highly integrated digital portals.

  • Dedicated ReBox App: Mobile app for residents to track drops, see environmental impact scores, and earn reward points.
  • Vendor Portal: App view for vendors to broadcast packaging needs (e.g., "Need 10 medium boxes") and view nearby drop-offs.
  • Admin & Guard Dashboard: Web console for society management and security guards to log/verify box pickups.

Evaluating ideas based on Desirability, Feasibility, and Viability.

To choose the best approach, we evaluated our main ideas against three primary Design Thinking criteria (scored 1 to 5):

💡 The Breakthrough: The Layered Solution

Rather than picking one technology tier, we decided to build a Layered Circular System. This allows users of all tech literacies (from app-averse seniors to app-native youth) to participate in the circular packaging economy.

4. The Layered Solution Design

How the physical and digital components work together.

Layer 1: The Basement Green Bin

The entry point for residents. By placing a physical, designated bin in apartment basements near garbage chutes, we make box disposal a zero-friction action. A clean, physical poster guides users on how to flatten the boxes and slide them in.

This serves users like Swarna, 45, who values simplicity and quick decluttering over downloading new digital tools.

Layer 2: The WhatsApp Bot Interface

When a resident scans the QR code on the Green Bin, it opens a WhatsApp chat. Through simple, interactive prompts, the resident can register their drop-off. In the background, the bot logs the box dimensions and increases their community impact score.

For security guards, the bot provides a quick verification system to log when a vendor retrieves boxes.

Layer 3: The Dedicated Dashboard (Future Expansion)

The high-fidelity portal that integrates all activities. It provides:

  • Online Shoppers: Track carbon offset metrics, tree-equivalent savings, and coordinate collection runs.
  • Local Shopkeepers: Broadcast requests for box sizes and locate the nearest apartment node with available stock.
  • Community Admins: Monitor total corrugated waste diverted from landfills and audit collection frequencies.

5. User Flows & Storyboard

Metamorphosis of cardboard waste into local value.

Our prototype models a seamless circular flow of boxes from resident to vendor:

Step User Action System Event Butterfly Analogy
1. Egg Resident receives Amazon parcel, extracts item. None (Eco-guilt and clutter begin to set in). Understand user need (Empathy).
2. Caterpillar Resident flattens box, takes it to basement basement bin. Resident scans Bin QR code to launch WhatsApp chat. Process inputs into actionable drop (Define).
3. Chrysalis Resident types box count, drops them inside the Green Bin. System logs drop, adds impact score, and updates inventory. Internal sorting and aggregation (Ideate).
4. Emergence Vendor (e.g., Ramesh Fruits) needs boxes, requests nearby sizes. WhatsApp Bot broadcasts "10 medium boxes ready" to registered local shopkeepers. Make the solution real & active (Prototype).
5. Flight Vendor picks up boxes from apartment security guard. Guard clicks "Verify Release" via WhatsApp, updating count. Solution takes flight in real-world circular loop (Test).

6. 7-Cycle Box Lifecycle Simulator

Interactive demonstration of box durability and cumulative eco-savings.

🖥️ Live Demonstration Utility

To showcase how a single corrugated box can survive 5 to 7 reuse cycles in a hyper-local circular loop, we designed an interactive ReBox Lifecycle Simulator. This tool demonstrates the progressive wear-and-tear of a box, its custody chain, and the ecological savings accumulated at each stage.

7. Validation & Pilot Testing Plan

A systematic framework to gather feedback on our interactive prototype.

We have structured our testing phase around a 2-week pilot in a local residential building (Bellandur Green Society, 200 apartments) and 5 nearby vendors:

Key Pilot Metrics

  • Divert Rate: % of e-commerce boxes redirected to the Green Bin vs municipal dry waste.
  • Vendor Adoption: Cost savings realized by shopkeepers ($ spent on new packaging vs second-hand boxes).
  • Friction Rate: % of users dropping off boxes who complete the WhatsApp Bot log vs dropping off anonymously.

Feedback Gathering Loops

  • Resident Interviews: Post-drop WhatsApp questionnaires to evaluate the convenience of QR scanning.
  • Vendor Checkins: In-person feedback on box cleanliness and logo acceptability during pickups.
  • Society Guard Feedback: Focus group with security guards to assess any operational burden during vendor handovers.
