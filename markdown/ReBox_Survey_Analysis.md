# ReBox – Survey Question Analysis
**Course:** MCA427DL – Design Thinking Lab | **Phase:** 2 – Empathy & Define  
**Purpose:** Evaluate whether the current survey questionnaires yield **actionable insights** or merely collect **surface-level data**

---

## Overall Verdict

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The current survey collects **mostly data, not deep insights**. Approximately 60–70% of the questions are framed as *"Would you [use our solution]?"* — that's **desirability testing** (Phase 5: Test), not empathy research (Phase 2).

The survey is a solid starting point but leans heavily toward quantitative data collection and solution validation rather than genuine empathy-driven insight discovery.

---

## ✅ Questions That Generate Real Insights

These questions tap into emotions, frustrations, and hidden behaviors — the gold standard for empathy research.

### Questionnaire 1 – Urban Resident / Online Shopper

| # | Question | Why It Works |
|---|----------|-------------|
| Q4 | *"Do you feel guilty throwing away a good-condition cardboard box? Why?"* | Taps into **emotional response** — guilt is a powerful motivator and reveals values |
| Q6 | *"What frustrates you the most about dealing with packaging waste in your flat or PG?"* | Uncovers **latent pain points** and unmet needs directly from the user's experience |

### Questionnaire 2 – Local Vendor / Small Business Owner

| # | Question | Why It Works |
|---|----------|-------------|
| Q4 | *"Are you satisfied with your current box supply in terms of cost, quality, and availability?"* | Opens up a **multi-dimensional pain point** — cost, quality, and supply chain issues |
| Q7 | *"What conditions would make you reject a donated box?"* | Reveals **hidden deal-breakers** and quality thresholds you might not have assumed |

### Questionnaire 3 – Apartment Complex / RWA Administrator

| # | Question | Why It Works |
|---|----------|-------------|
| Q2 | *"Is cardboard and packaging waste a growing problem…especially with quick-commerce?"* | Contextualizes the problem in the **user's lived reality** |
| Q5 | *"What is your biggest concern about placing such a bin?"* | Surfaces **real blockers** — not hypothetical objections, but genuine fears |
| Q10 | *"Are there any BBMP regulations, apartment by-laws, or facility management concerns that could block such an initiative?"* | Uncovers **systemic barriers** that could kill the entire project |

## ⚠️ Questions That Collect Data, Not Insights

These questions yield counts, yes/no answers, or hypothetical willingness — useful for reports but not for understanding **why** people behave the way they do.

### Problem 1: Closed / Leading Questions

| Questionnaire | Question | Issue |
|---|----------|-------|
| Q1.8 | *"If a drop-off bin was placed in your apartment basement…would you use it?"* | Binary Yes/No — you get a **count**, not a **story** |
| Q1.9 | *"Would you scan a QR code to log your drop-off, or is that too much effort?"* | Leading — implies the answer and limits exploration |
| Q1.11 | *"How comfortable are you using a mobile app for something like this?"* | Scale question that reveals **preference**, not **behavior** |
| Q2.5 | *"Would you use second-hand Amazon/Flipkart/Myntra boxes if they are clean and intact?"* | Hypothetical — everyone says "yes" in surveys |
| Q3.4 | *"Would you allow a dedicated ReBox collection bin…?"* | Hypothetical willingness ≠ actual permission |

### Problem 2: Solution-First Framing (Belongs in Phase 5, Not Phase 2)

| Questionnaire | Question | Issue |
|---|----------|-------|
| Q1.9 | *"Would you scan a QR code…?"* | You're **testing your prototype** before finishing empathy |
| Q1.10 | *"What would motivate you to participate — reward points, UPI cashback…?"* | Presents **your ideas** as options instead of letting users express their own motivations |
| Q2.10 | *"Would you be willing to use an app or WhatsApp bot to place box requests?"* | Tests a **specific solution channel** — premature at this stage |
| Q3.8 | *"Would your RWA officially support and promote ReBox…via MyGate/NoBrokerHood?"* | Asks about **your distribution plan**, not their actual communication habits |

### Problem 3: Hypothetical Willingness (The Say-Do Gap)

| Questionnaire | Question | Issue |
|---|----------|-------|
| Q1.7 | *"Would you be comfortable donating used boxes…?"* | People **say** yes but **don't act** — the classic Say-Do gap |
| Q1.12 | *"Would you recommend such a service to your flatmates…?"* | Hypothetical referral ≠ actual word-of-mouth behavior |
| Q3.9 | *"Would a monthly report on boxes collected…be useful…?"* | Nobody says "no" to free reports — gives no actionable signal |

### Problem 4: Surface-Level Behavioral Data

| Questionnaire | Question | Issue |
|---|----------|-------|
| Q1.1 | *"How many online deliveries do you receive per month?"* | Market sizing data — useful, but tells you nothing about **why** |
| Q1.3 | *"How long does the box stay in your home…?"* | Factual metric — no emotional or behavioral depth |
| Q2.1 | *"How many cardboard boxes do you use per week…?"* | Volume data — helps quantify, not empathize |
| Q2.2 | *"How much do you currently spend on cardboard boxes per week?"* | Cost data — useful for pricing, not for understanding needs |
| Q2.8 | *"What box sizes do you need most frequently?"* | Logistics data — belongs in a product requirements doc |

## 🔄 Recommended Replacements: Data → Insight Questions

Transform surface-level questions into story-driven, open-ended prompts that unlock genuine empathy.

### For Urban Residents (Questionnaire 1)

| Instead Of | Ask This |
|-----------|----------|
| *"Would you use a drop-off bin?"* | **"Walk me through what happens from the moment a delivery arrives to when the box finally leaves your home."** |
| *"Would you scan a QR code?"* | **"What's the laziest thing you've ever done to get rid of packaging waste? No judgment."** |
| *"What would motivate you to participate?"* | **"Think of the last time you went out of your way to do something good for the environment. What made you do it?"** |
| *"Would you recommend this to flatmates?"* | **"Have you ever argued with a flatmate or family member about clutter or waste? Tell me about it."** |

### For Local Vendors (Questionnaire 2)

| Instead Of | Ask This |
|-----------|----------|
| *"Would you use second-hand boxes?"* | **"Tell me about the last time you needed a box urgently. Where did you go? What was that experience like?"** |
| *"Would you use an app or WhatsApp bot?"* | **"When you run low on boxes mid-day, what do you actually do? Walk me through it step by step."** |
| *"What is the maximum you'd pay per reused box?"* | **"What's the most annoying thing about buying boxes right now — is it the cost, the quality, finding the right size, or something else entirely?"** |

### For RWA Administrators (Questionnaire 3)

| Instead Of | Ask This |
|-----------|----------|
| *"Would your RWA support and promote ReBox?"* | **"What's the last sustainability initiative your apartment tried? What happened to it — did it succeed or fizzle out? Why?"** |
| *"Would you allow a dedicated ReBox bin?"* | **"What's the biggest waste-related complaint you've gotten from residents in the last 6 months?"** |
| *"Would a monthly report be useful?"* | **"When you need to convince your management committee to approve something, what kind of evidence works best?"** |

## 💡 Key Recommendations

1. **Reframe 5–6 closed questions** per questionnaire into open-ended, story-driven prompts
2. **Move solution-testing questions** (QR codes, apps, WhatsApp bots, ReBox bins) to a **separate Phase 5 validation survey**
3. **Use the survey as a conversation guide** for 1-on-1 interviews, not as a standalone written form — the best insights come from follow-up questions like *"Tell me more about that"* and *"Why?"*
4. **Add observation notes** — in Design Thinking, what people *do* matters more than what they *say*. Add a section for interviewer observations (body language, hesitation, environment)
5. **Include a "surprise me" question** at the end of each questionnaire:  
   *"Is there anything about cardboard waste / box reuse / packaging that I should have asked but didn't?"*
